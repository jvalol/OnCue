Changelog

OnCue is free. Every release is here, newest first, and the latest one is on the front page. There's an RSS feed if you'd rather be told.

4.22

21 October 2026

OnCue can be used without seeing it. Every control in the popover and in Preferences now has a name a screen reader can say: the two Trigger boxes say which is sound and which is motion, the sliders and meters say what they measure, and the meters say their level as a percentage. In Preferences each pop-up and field is named by the label beside it, and the schedule's weekday boxes say the whole day. The same names serve Voice Control, Switch Control and the keyboard. The one gap is drawing zones in the preview window, which still takes a mouse; the help says so.

4.21

12 October 2026

If OnCue is set to arm itself when it opens but not to open at login, a restart leaves it closed and nothing arms. The popover now says so until Open OnCue at Login is turned on, the way it has for the schedule since 4.14. If you installed from this site or through Homebrew before 4.8, this is likely you: the login item didn't survive that update's change of identity, and the box has read off since.

The popover keeps its glass but no longer goes white over a light window. It tints itself halfway toward the window colour, so the text reads whatever is behind it. And it closes when a click in it opens the browser or Finder, since what you asked for is what should be in front.

4.17

30 September 2026

Make a Time-lapse, in the popover menu, turns the last stretch of being armed into one short movie: each recording's still, then a frame every two seconds of it, in order across both cameras, four frames a second, with the time in the corner and the camera's name when there are two. A thirty-second recording becomes four seconds; a busy afternoon, about a minute. It lands in the recordings folder as "What happened", named for the stretch, opens in Finder when it's done, and is never touched by the size limit. Making it again for the same stretch replaces the last one.

Show What Happened and Make a Time-lapse now say which period they mean, right in the menu, so once you've armed again there's no guessing.

Every recording now comes with a still: a JPEG under the same name, taken at the moment the recording was triggered. 4.13

23 September 2026

OnCue works with Shortcuts, Siri and Focus. There are four actions: Arm OnCue, Disarm OnCue, Is OnCue Armed, and Record a Test. And there's a Focus Filter: add it to a Focus in System Settings, turn on its one switch, and OnCue arms itself whenever that Focus is on and disarms when it ends. Because a Focus follows you across your devices, an Away Focus that your iPhone turns on when you leave home arms the Mac before you're down the street. Nothing is sent anywhere; the Focus is Apple's, and OnCue still has no network permission. A Focus only disarms what it armed, so arming it yourself still means what it meant. It can only arm OnCue while OnCue is open, so turn on Open OnCue at Login with it.

If OnCue quits unexpectedly while armed, a crash or a force quit or a power cut, the next launch says so, names the time, and arms again. Until now that was the one way of not watching that never told you.

Record a Test, in the menu, records on every camera the way a trigger would, shows you the file, and puts things back how they were. It's the quick way to see a setup work without waiting for something to happen, and if anything about the setup is wrong, this is where it says so.

4.12

23 September 2026

A schedule now keeps the Mac awake the whole time it's on, days without a window included. Before this, OnCue held off sleep only while armed, so a Mac that slept between windows couldn't notice the next one start; a weekday schedule missed Monday morning unless someone woke the Mac first. Nothing an app can do wakes a sleeping Mac, so it doesn't let it sleep. Between windows the popover says when the next one starts.

The line under the arm state that counts recent recordings now says outright when they're barely longer than the shortest OnCue can make, which is what the two numbers were there to let you work out.

4.10

23 September 2026

You can tell OnCue where to look. In the preview window, drag a rectangle around the part of the picture that matters, the door rather than the whole room, and motion outside it isn't counted. Draw as many as you need, click one to select it, drag it to move it, press Delete to remove it, or clear them all with the button. The rest of the picture is dimmed so you can see what's being ignored, and movement there still shows; it just doesn't start a recording. Each camera has its own zones, and recordings are still the whole frame.

The preview shows movement as a red tint over the picture now, instead of painting it solid red, so you can see what's actually moving. Detection is unchanged. OnCue also skips the preview picture entirely while nothing is showing it, which is most of the time, and the preview window no longer opens with black bars above and below the picture when OnCue is already armed.

It arms at the start with the same countdown and notification as arming at login, disarms at the end, and if you disarm it yourself during a window it stays disarmed until the next one starts. Anything you arm by hand is left alone. The schedule only runs while OnCue is open, so turn on Open OnCue at Login with it.

4.8

22 September 2026

Read this one before you update. OnCue is on the Mac App Store again with this release, and to make that work it had to change its identity under the hood. macOS treats the updated app as new, so after this update: OnCue will not open at login until you turn that back on, and the old entry has to go first. Open System Settings, General, Login Items, remove the OnCue row that's there, then in OnCue's Preferences tick Open OnCue at Login again. Arm at login, your recordings folder, your thresholds and the size limit are back at their defaults too, so go through Preferences once. Your recordings are exactly where they were. macOS will ask about the camera and microphone again the first time you arm. I'm sorry about all of it; it happens once.

OnCue now says when it's starting with default settings, in the popover, until you've opened Preferences or armed it. Recordings are named from the footage's own clock rather than the Mac's.

4.7.1

21 September 2026

Settings is honest about the recordings folder now. It would let you choose a folder OnCue can't write to, a Time Machine disk being the one that caught me, and say nothing until recordings started failing while I was away. It checks when you choose, and won't take one it can't use.

It also kept naming a drive after I ejected it, even after closing and reopening Settings, while recordings were quietly going to your Movies folder instead. It now tells you where they're actually going, names the folder you chose when that isn't available, and says when the folder is there but can't be written to.

4.7

21 September 2026

If you keep recordings on an external drive, this release makes OnCue work for you at all. There was a bug. It was asking macOS how much room was left and getting back zero from any drive that isn't the built-in one, so it refused to record and told you the disk was full. It never even tried to write. Nothing you did caused it and nothing on your drive was wrong.

The size limit now applies while a recording is running, not just between recordings. A recording keeps going for as long as something keeps moving, so one of them could run all night and take the folder well past a limit you'd set. Now it carries on in a new file instead, picking up exactly where the last one left off, and the one that closed can be cleared like any other. Running out of space ends the recording cleanly and says so, rather than writing until it fails and losing the end of it.

The popover also tells you how many recordings the last 24 hours produced and how long they typically run. The shortest OnCue makes by default is 10s. The maximum is 630s. Anything between is configurable.

4.6

21 September 2026

OnCue now says when it has stopped watching for motion. Detection could fail while everything looks healthy, camera running, etc, and yet nothing records. It could be for a variety of reasons, but without any kind of indication the point of a security system is flawed if it's giving false positive signals about its readiness. The popover says as much, that camera's meter greys out, and Arm is unavailable while motion is the only thing set to trigger. If it recovers, OnCue tells you how long it missed. Sound carries on watching throughout, and an OnCue that's already armed stays armed.

4.5.2

21 September 2026

Fixes for things that could go wrong while nobody was watching. Unplug the drive you record to and OnCue goes back to your Movies folder for the next recording, rather than failing until you restart it. New recordings are readable only by you, and a folder OnCue makes for itself is private, which matters if you keep recordings somewhere shared. The size limit only deletes files now: a folder that happened to be named like a recording could have gone with everything inside it.

4.5.1

21 September 2026

A fix for anyone installing OnCue for the first time. Until now, the recordings from that first session went into your Movies folder rather than the OnCue folder inside it, so Show Recordings opened an empty folder and the size limit had nothing to count. They go to the right place now, and any that landed loose are still in Movies where you left them.

4.5

21 September 2026

OnCue won't fill your disk any more. It stops before the last of the space is gone and says so, rather than recording into a full disk and failing. If you'd rather it kept going, Settings takes a number of GB to keep, and OnCue deletes its own oldest recordings to stay under it. That's off unless you set it, it never touches a file OnCue didn't write, and renaming a recording keeps it for good.

4.4

20 September 2026

Settings can be set to no microphone, so OnCue watches without listening. Recording a room is one thing and recording what's said in it is another, and until now the second was only avoidable by unplugging something. Choosing no microphone, or no camera, also switches off the trigger that depended on it, rather than leaving a tick behind for a device you just removed.

4.3

20 September 2026

A recording that can't be saved now says so, instead of failing quietly while OnCue carried on looking like it was working. The menu bar icon turns yellow and the popover says why: no room left, the folder gone, or OnCue not allowed to write there. It stays until you disarm, so it's still there when you come back, and it says how many recordings in a row have gone the same way. OnCue keeps watching either way.

4.2

20 September 2026

The meters now colour themselves against your own trigger rather than a fixed level: green while what they're picking up wouldn't start a recording, yellow as it gets close, red once it would. Before this, a meter could sit red while nothing was going to happen, or stay green with a recording about to start. And if a recording loses frames, its name says how many seconds are missing, so a thin file can't quietly pass for a complete one.

4.1

20 September 2026

OnCue only holds the camera while it's actually watching now. Unarmed with nothing open, the camera is off and its light in the menu bar is out, so a lit camera means OnCue is watching rather than just running. If macOS is lighting your screen to brighten what the camera sees, OnCue says so, since that lights the room it's meant to be quietly watching and OnCue can't switch it off itself. Settings says which picker is which, and you can now choose no camera at all and let sound do the watching.

4.0

19 September 2026

The first release in a long time, and most of OnCue was new. Sound can set off a recording, on its own or alongside motion, and each trigger has its own meter and threshold. Two cameras can watch at once, each recording its own file. Every recording reaches back a few seconds before whatever started it, so the thing you wanted to see isn't already over. Recordings are named for when they started and what recorded them, and they go wherever you point them. OnCue keeps your Mac awake while it's armed, says something when closing the lid would stop it, and can open and arm itself after a restart.
